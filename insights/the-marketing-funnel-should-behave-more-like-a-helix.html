The Marketing Funnel Should Behave More Like a Helix

The traditional funnel taught me how to think about progression. The community flywheel showed me how relationships, advocacy, and referrals can generate continuing growth momentum. But in startup and service-business work, I needed a model that could represent repeated interaction and forward movement at the same time.

By Neri Published 5 October 2026 Applied lifecycle + relationship marketing
Tags Lifecycle · Brand · Operations · Partnerships
Framework Acquire → Progress → Convert → Experience → Re-engage → Advocate → Refer
The short answer

A funnel organizes progression. A flywheel explains recurring growth momentum. A helix adds direction to repeated relationship cycles.

For startups and service businesses, customers can come around repeatedly without starting over. Each meaningful interaction should carry more trust, context, experience, and relationship value into the next one.

01

The funnel and flywheel are useful, but they answer different questions

The helix came from needing progression and recurrence in the same model.

I first learned the standard marketing funnel: awareness, consideration, intent, and conversion.

It remains useful because it gives marketing a framework for understanding what has to happen before someone becomes a customer.

I also learned the community flywheel, where customers, relationships, advocacy, referrals, and community participation can continue generating growth momentum.

I do not see that momentum as unproven. The issue for me was representation.

In service businesses and startups still building a name, I kept seeing people return to the business while also moving forward in familiarity, confidence, and commitment.

Funnel

Organizes progression toward an outcome and helps measure movement between stages.

Flywheel

Explains how recurring customer, community, and advocacy activity can sustain growth momentum.

Helix

Represents repeated interaction that also moves the relationship forward with accumulated value.

The advantage of the helix is not that it eliminates repetition. It turns repetition into progression.

02

Trust in the relationship is the bread and butter of service businesses and startups

Each rotation should leave the next interaction with more trust to work with.

A service is often difficult to evaluate before someone experiences it. The customer may be choosing while dealing with uncertainty, risk, or a problem they cannot solve alone.

A startup has another version of the same challenge. It may not yet have years of recognition, institutional reputation, or accumulated market familiarity.

Relationships help close that gap.

The first interaction may create recognition. Another can create familiarity. Useful content can demonstrate competence. A conversation can build confidence. Service delivery creates direct experience.

This is why a returning customer or prospect should not be treated as though nothing happened before.

The same principle appears in When Search Becomes the Verification Layer . Someone may already know the business and use search not for discovery, but to verify whether that existing impression deserves more trust.

For service businesses and startups, the most valuable thing moving through the helix may not simply be the customer. It is the trust accumulated around the relationship.

03

A customer can loop without moving backward

Returning to the business does not mean returning to awareness.

A customer journey can look like:

Search. Leave. See content. Return. Ask a question. Pause. Receive follow-up. Search the brand. Use a tool. Submit a form. Convert.

That person did not move neatly down a cone.

They repeatedly encountered the business while their state changed.

A circle comes back to the same point. A helix comes around again at a different level.

That distinction also explains why the same channel can serve different purposes. Search can create discovery or verification. Email can nurture a prospect or re-engage an existing relationship. Chat can answer an early question or move a high-intent visitor into intake.

As I discussed in Why the Best Call to Action Depends on Where the Customer Is in the Journey , the next action should reflect what the customer is ready to do, not merely where the business wants them to end up.

One way to think about the helical journey
↻
Awareness

The customer encounters the name, problem, or service.

↻
Recognition

The business becomes familiar rather than completely new.

↻
Engagement

The customer reads, watches, asks, clicks, chats, or returns.

↻
Trust and verification

The customer gathers evidence that the business may be credible and relevant.

↻
Qualification and conversion

The relationship moves into a higher level of commitment.

↻
Experience

The customer now has direct evidence of what the relationship is like.

↻
Advocacy and referral

Accumulated trust can begin influencing another customer relationship.

04

Conversion should redirect the journey, not end it

A former client should not be recycled into awareness.

A customer who has completed the service already possesses something a cold prospect does not: direct experience.

They know the business. They have a trust history. They understand what interaction with the organization feels like.

Sending that person back through generic awareness marketing ignores accumulated relationship equity.

Their path should instead redirect toward:

Post-conversion redirection
Experience → Relationship → Re-engagement → Advocacy → Referral

This builds on Why the Lead Is Not the Conversion . Conversion is an important outcome, but it is not the only point at which marketing can create value.

Conversion should change the customer's path, not terminate the relationship.

05

Re-engagement is a relationship structure inside remarketing

Remarketing should be broader than showing ads to people who already know you.

Retargeting ads can be useful, but they are only one mechanism.

In a relationship-led model, remarketing asks a larger question:

What does this person already know about us, and what should happen next because of that history?

Re-engagement therefore sits within remarketing as a way to continue a relationship that has become quiet, paused, or inactive.

That continuation could involve useful content, email, SMS, a direct check-in, a service update, a review request, a referral opportunity, community participation, or paid retargeting when appropriate.

The mechanism matters less than the continuity.

This is also why A CRM Should Preserve Context, Not Just Contact Data matters. If the organization cannot remember whether someone is a new prospect, former client, inactive lead, referrer, or advocate, it cannot continue the relationship intelligently.

Re-engagement is not a return to awareness. It is a continuation from the point where the relationship previously stopped.

06

Word of mouth is where accumulated trust begins to travel

One relationship can change the starting position of the next.

Advocacy is not simply what happens when someone happens to like a business.

For a service business, it should be part of the lifecycle design.

A satisfied former client can review, recommend, refer, answer questions about their experience, or keep the business present within their own network.

When they refer someone, the new person may not enter as a completely cold prospect.

They may arrive with borrowed credibility: someone they trust already trusts the business.

This connects directly to the relationship principle behind Is Direct Marketing Irrelevant in the Age of AI? and to the trust-building role of familiar, useful interactions in Why Community Events Should Solve a Relationship Problem .

Word of mouth is how trust accumulated in one customer relationship can become an input into the next customer relationship.

07

Each rotation can carry greater magnitude

The business should enter the next cycle with more relationship value than before.

This is where I ultimately side with the helix.

The community flywheel explains how recurring activity can build momentum. The helix adds a visible direction to that recurrence.

A startup or service business can enter the next rotation with more than it had in the previous one:

more recognition, more customer experience, more reviews, more referrals, more evidence, more context, and more people willing to advocate for the business.

The customer progresses, but the organization can progress too.

That does not mean every rotation will produce exponential growth or that every customer becomes an advocate.

It means the system should be designed so that useful relationship value is not discarded after each transaction.

The helical relationship framework
Acquire → Progress → Convert → Experience → Re-engage → Advocate → Refer

The journey should repeat, but the relationship should never restart. Every meaningful interaction should leave the next one with more trust to work with.

08

A helix only works if marketing preserves what each rotation creates

Trust has to survive the handoff between channels, systems, and people.

The model becomes operational when marketing can preserve context across the journey.

Calls to action need to reflect readiness. That is the operating problem behind Call-to-Action Hierarchy Is a Lead Capture Strategy .

CRM and automation need to know what happened before.

Content needs to help customers solve the next question rather than repeatedly introducing the business.

Post-service communication needs to recognize that the person is now a former client, not a stranger.

Advocacy needs a legitimate path into reviews, referrals, and word of mouth.

The helix therefore is not only a diagram of the customer journey. It is a framework for deciding how marketing should treat a relationship as it changes over time.

Scope

What this Insight does and does not claim

I use the helix as an applied marketing framework, particularly for startups and service businesses where trust, repeated interaction, re-engagement, referrals, and customer relationships carry substantial value.

It is not a claim that the traditional funnel is obsolete or that the community flywheel does not create growth momentum.

The funnel remains useful for organizing and measuring progression. The flywheel remains useful for understanding recurring momentum.

The helix is useful when I need to represent another property: customers can repeatedly encounter a business while moving forward with accumulated trust and context, and completed relationships can contribute to the starting conditions of future ones.

Experience note. This framework developed from applying funnel, lifecycle, relationship, word-of-mouth, re-engagement, and service-business marketing principles across actual work. It is presented as an operating model for thinking about customer progression, not as a universal mathematical model of customer behavior.
