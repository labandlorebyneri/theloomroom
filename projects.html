Market Research for Word-of-Mouth Strategy

A multi-volume market research and marketing strategy project for a regional multi-location service business, examining how an existing relationship-led audience consumes media and how those patterns could inform word-of-mouth growth, content, channels, search, and measurement.

Research was treated as the beginning of the decision process, not the end. Subsequent performance was used to test and refine the original strategic recommendations.

Content Channels Lifecycle Operations Search

Research · Analysis · Strategy · Measurement

Research Design

A research-to-validation framework for a relationship-led growth strategy.

The objective was to understand how an existing relationship-led audience uses media, where interests overlap, and which patterns were strong enough to become practical marketing hypotheses. The research was designed to inform content, channel, search, lifecycle, and measurement decisions without treating self-reported audience behavior as proof of future performance.

163 valid responses
50 reported ZIP codes
Nov–Dec 2025 field period
11 mo. performance validation
01

Objective

Identify media habits, content interests, and audience overlaps that could improve a word-of-mouth-led marketing system, then translate those observations into testable decisions rather than fixed audience assumptions.

Decision areas: content · channels · search · lifecycle · measurement
02

Sampling and scope

The study used 163 voluntary responses recruited through established relationship networks. Because the sample was non-random, the findings describe the recruited audience and were not treated as representative estimates for a wider population.

Voluntary · non-random · relationship-based recruitment
03

Analytical method

Descriptive shares were combined with respondent-level association testing. Multi-select responses were analyzed as overlapping behaviors, with contingency tables used to distinguish high-count combinations from relationships that were stronger than expected by chance.

Phi · lift · two-sided Fisher exact tests · Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
04

Validation design

The research produced hypotheses, not permanent rules. Subsequent marketing performance was reviewed across search, content, social, paid media, email, CRM, and related execution to determine what the original strategy got right, what changed, and what still required better evidence.

Research → hypothesis → execution → evidence → revision

Selected Findings

Go deeper into the evidence.

These selected findings show how the research moved from audience observation to strategic testing, and how later performance evidence challenged or reinforced the original direction.

01

Audience Research

The foundation study examined audience behavior, media overlap, content affinity, and the strategic questions those patterns created.

Selected findings
  • Facebook was selected by 85.3% of respondents, but substantial platform overlap meant those percentages could not be treated as separate pools of unique reach.
  • 66.3% reported using free ad-supported streaming services, creating a media-testing opportunity without proving actual advertising exposure.
  • Sports programming and sports websites produced the strongest measured show-to-website association in the study.
  • Several content relationships survived multiple-testing correction, while other high-count combinations did not, reinforcing the distinction between popularity and meaningful association.
  • The survey did not establish a content-specific best posting hour, so timing remained a hypothesis to test rather than a permanent rule.
02

Performance Lookback

Eleven months of subsequent marketing performance were used to test the assumptions that came out of the original research.

Selected findings
  • Search visibility was already substantial, but high-intent commercial pages captured only a small share of available clicks.
  • Social impressions remained comparatively resilient despite substantially lower posting volume, while engagement and click behavior weakened.
  • Paid-social web conversions increased from 22 to 99 while overall spending remained broadly similar.
  • Actual content performance challenged the original timing hypothesis, with stronger observed results later in the afternoon and evening.
  • Of 19 strategic claims reviewed, 8 were affirmed, 5 challenged, 2 produced mixed evidence, and 4 were not yet testable.
