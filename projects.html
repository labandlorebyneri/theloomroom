Audience Research · Strategy · Measurement

Market Research for Word-of-Mouth Strategy

A multi-volume market research and marketing strategy project for a regional multi-location service business, examining how an existing relationship-led audience consumes media and how those patterns could inform word-of-mouth growth, content, channels, search, and measurement.

The brief

Understand how the organization’s existing audience uses social platforms, websites, streaming services, audio, entertainment, and local cultural references, then turn those findings into practical marketing direction.

The research

The study examined which platforms, services, websites, show categories, sports interests, and media habits were common within the audience.

The analysis went beyond simple percentages by testing which interests actually traveled together, accounting for people who selected many options, examining audience groupings, and identifying where the evidence was strong enough to guide a marketing decision.

The strategy

Findings were translated into content pillars, channel roles, local-market direction, search priorities, distinctive brand assets, media-buying questions, measurement principles, and a portfolio of tests.

The lookback

Eleven months of search, social, paid media, website, email, CRM, and content performance were later compared against the original recommendations.

Each major claim was classified as affirmed, challenged, mixed, or not yet testable, with challenged findings turned into revised hypotheses and new tests.

Research was treated as the beginning of the decision process, not the end. The recommendations remained open to revision when actual audience behavior disagreed.

Search · Strategy · Commercial Opportunity

Organic Search Opportunity Analysis

A 16-month Google Search Console analysis examining the gap between commercial search visibility and actual click capture for a regional multi-location service business.

The problem

The site was already receiving meaningful visibility for commercially valuable searches, particularly queries associated with high-intent service needs.

The problem was that pages most closely connected to qualified leads and signed business were capturing only a small portion of that visibility as traffic.

The diagnosis

The analysis separated visibility from performance and identified several different issues operating at the same time: weak click capture, overlapping URLs, inconsistent intent ownership, uneven commercial messaging, geographic targeting questions, and limited internal support for priority service pages.

The action plan

Recommendations prioritized search-result messaging, above-the-fold commercial relevance, geographic signals, internal linking, URL ownership, and clearer separation between local and statewide search intent.

The plan also separated SEO leading indicators from downstream business outcomes so search improvements could be evaluated both technically and commercially.

The immediate opportunity was not creating search demand from zero. It was capturing more value from commercial visibility the site already had.
