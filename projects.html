Market Research for Word-of-Mouth Strategy

A multi-volume market research and marketing strategy project for a regional multi-location service business, examining how an existing relationship-led audience consumes media and how those patterns could inform word-of-mouth growth, content, channels, search, and measurement.

Research was treated as the beginning of the decision process, not the end. Subsequent performance was used to test and refine the original strategic recommendations.

Content Channels Lifecycle Operations Search

Research · Analysis · Strategy · Measurement

Research Design

A research-to-validation framework for a relationship-led growth strategy.

The objective was to understand how an existing relationship-led audience uses media, where interests overlap, and which patterns were strong enough to become practical marketing hypotheses. The research was designed to inform content, channel, search, lifecycle, and measurement decisions without treating self-reported audience behavior as proof of future performance.

163 valid responses
50 reported ZIP codes
Nov–Dec 2025 field period
11 mo. performance validation
01

Objective

Identify media habits, content interests, and audience overlaps that could improve a word-of-mouth-led marketing system, then translate those observations into testable decisions rather than fixed audience assumptions.

Decision areas: content · channels · search · lifecycle · measurement
02

Sampling and scope

The study used 163 voluntary responses recruited through established relationship networks. Because the sample was non-random, the findings describe the recruited audience and were not treated as representative estimates for a wider population.

Voluntary · non-random · relationship-based recruitment
03

Analytical method

Descriptive shares were combined with respondent-level association testing. Multi-select responses were analyzed as overlapping behaviors, with contingency tables used to distinguish high-count combinations from relationships that were stronger than expected by chance.

Phi · lift · two-sided Fisher exact tests · Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
04

Validation design

The research produced hypotheses, not permanent rules. Subsequent marketing performance was reviewed across search, content, social, paid media, email, CRM, and related execution to determine what the original strategy got right, what changed, and what still required better evidence.

Research → hypothesis → execution → evidence → revision

Selected Findings

Go deeper into the evidence.

These selected findings show how the research moved from audience observation to strategic testing, and how later performance evidence challenged or reinforced the original direction.

01

Audience Research

The foundation study examined audience behavior, media overlap, content affinity, and the strategic questions those patterns created.

Selected findings
  • Facebook was selected by 85.3% of respondents, but substantial platform overlap meant those percentages could not be treated as separate pools of unique reach.
  • 66.3% reported using free ad-supported streaming services, creating a media-testing opportunity without proving actual advertising exposure.
  • Sports programming and sports websites produced the strongest measured show-to-website association in the study.
  • Several content relationships survived multiple-testing correction, while other high-count combinations did not, reinforcing the distinction between popularity and meaningful association.
  • The survey did not establish a content-specific best posting hour, so timing remained a hypothesis to test rather than a permanent rule.
02

Performance Lookback

Eleven months of subsequent marketing performance were used to test the assumptions that came out of the original research.

Selected findings
  • Search visibility was already substantial, but high-intent commercial pages captured only a small share of available clicks.
  • Social impressions remained comparatively resilient despite substantially lower posting volume, while engagement and click behavior weakened.
  • Paid-social web conversions increased from 22 to 99 while overall spending remained broadly similar.
  • Actual content performance challenged the original timing hypothesis, with stronger observed results later in the afternoon and evening.
  • Of 19 strategic claims reviewed, 8 were affirmed, 5 challenged, 2 produced mixed evidence, and 4 were not yet testable.

One Brand Experience Project

An ongoing brand-system and customer-experience project for Chris Corzo Injury Attorneys, moving from fragmented marketing expressions toward a connected system for how the Call Corzo identity is expressed across creative, content, communication, conversion, community, and customer touchpoints.

Brand consistency does not require every asset to look identical. It requires every asset to be recognizably connected to the same identity.

Brand Content Channels Creative Lifecycle Operations

Brand Strategy · Creative Systems · Customer Experience · Governance

One Brand Experience

From an established digital identity to one connected brand system.

The One Brand Experience Project began in February 2025 with a question that initially appeared visual: why did the same firm sometimes feel like a different brand depending on where someone encountered it?

Chris Corzo Injury Attorneys already had a recognizable digital identity. Its website established a strong visual and verbal foundation around Chris Corzo, a red, blue, navy, and white palette, prominent service messaging, clear calls to action, and the promise of Maximum Care, Maximum Recovery, and Maximum Speed.

Outside that core digital experience, however, the brand was not always expressed with the same consistency. Social media graphics, campaign materials, email communications, promotional assets, community content, and other creative executions could follow different conventions or communicate the firm in ways that did not clearly reinforce the identity customers encountered elsewhere.

What began as an effort to create greater visual alignment became a broader exercise in understanding how one brand could remain recognizable across many different forms of communication.

System Development

One identity, adapted to different jobs.

The objective is not visual repetition. It is recognizable continuity. Each execution can respond to its own message, audience, channel, and technical requirements while still belonging unmistakably to the same parent brand.

01

Brand Derivatives

Different communication requirements are treated as related derivatives of the same identity rather than isolated creative jobs.

Developing system
  • The red, blue, navy, and white identity provides a recognizable visual foundation without requiring identical compositions.
  • Service-line, result, authority, community, seasonal, and values-led content can use different visual intensity while remaining connected.
  • Recurring typography, imagery, logo treatment, composition, messaging, and conversion conventions help create continuity.
  • Successful executions can become reusable patterns rather than one-off solutions.
  • New formats are treated as opportunities to discover which elements should remain fixed and which can adapt.
02

Connected Customer Experience

The project expanded once the brand was understood as something customers experience across multiple interactions rather than only something they see in creative assets.

Experience principle
  • A social interaction may become a branded search, website visit, case review, chat interaction, email, or direct conversation.
  • Search, website content, conversion tools, and follow-up should reinforce rather than contradict the expectations created upstream.
  • Accessibility and speed become operational brand questions when the brand explicitly promises care and responsiveness.
  • Community initiatives and values-led communication help extend the brand beyond immediate commercial messages.
  • The customer experiences the organization as one brand even when the underlying touchpoints are produced through different systems.

One Brand, Many Expressions

Variation is necessary. Disconnection is not.

A website can be formal and conversion-focused. A social campaign can be playful. A case-result graphic can be forceful. A community initiative can be warm. A values-led message can be reflective. A billboard can be radically simple.

What matters is that each expression reinforces the same underlying identity and contributes to the same customer experience.

The continuing work of the One Brand Experience Project is the discovery, development, and refinement of those relationships: determining how the Call Corzo identity can remain coherent while becoming flexible enough to operate across every meaningful expression of the brand.

One identity. Many expressions. One connected experience.

From TikTok Lawyer to a Broader Public Identity

A rebranding, social media, and public-relations engagement for Atty. Tony Roman during a period when his public identity was expanding beyond the “TikTok Lawyer” persona. The work focused on introducing more dimensions of the person behind an already recognizable digital identity while preserving the familiarity and accessibility that had helped build his audience.

A successful rebrand does not erase the identity people already recognize. It gives that recognition somewhere new to go.

Brand Content Channels PR & Events Creative Operations

Rebranding · Social Media Strategy · Public Relations · Content Development · Creative Coordination

Public Identity Repositioning

Expanding a recognizable digital persona into a broader public identity.

When I joined Atty. Tony Roman’s team in June 2022, the “TikTok Lawyer” identity already gave the public an immediate way to recognize him. The challenge was not to replace that recognition. It was to broaden what people understood about the person behind it.

My work as Social Media Manager with public-relations responsibilities became part of a larger repositioning effort. Social content, visual presentation, messaging, and public-facing communication were used to introduce more dimensions of his professional and public identity while keeping the accessibility that made the original platform recognizable.

The project took place before his later congressional career. This case study focuses on the branding and communications problem itself: how an established platform identity can become an entry point into a broader public profile without discarding the recognition already built around it.

Repositioning Strategy

Recognition had to transfer, not disappear.

The strategic task was to use an existing, memorable identity as the starting point for a broader public introduction rather than treating rebranding as a visual reset.

01

From Persona to Person

The “TikTok Lawyer” identity remained useful because it gave audiences an immediate point of recognition. The next layer of communication had to give that recognition more context.

Strategic direction
  • Preserve the accessibility associated with the established digital persona.
  • Introduce more dimensions of the person behind the platform identity.
  • Use repeated public-facing communication to build familiarity beyond a single content format.
  • Keep visual and verbal signals connected enough that the broader identity still felt recognizable.
  • Allow the existing persona to function as an entry point rather than a limitation.
02

Social Media & PR as One System

The work required more than publishing posts. Social content and public relations had to reinforce the same evolving public identity.

Operating principle
  • Content development supported both regular audience engagement and broader public introduction.
  • Creative decisions had to reinforce recognition while allowing the identity to expand.
  • Public-facing messages needed consistency across different topics and communication moments.
  • Production and publishing rhythms had to support sustained visibility rather than isolated bursts.
  • The communication system worked best when brand, content, channel, and PR decisions were treated as connected work.

An end-to-end local campaign project for the political slate of mayoralty candidate Renato Matawaran in Dinalupihan, Bataan, combining constituent research, advocacy development, campaign communication, field activation, event staging, production, and post-production.

Community engagement was treated not only as an activation channel, but as an input into message development, advocacy priorities, and subsequent campaign communication.

Campaign System

From community listening to campaign execution.

The project connected research, message development, community consultation, campaign production, field activation, and post-production within one operating system. Rather than separating listening from communication, information gathered through direct engagement helped shape the advocacy priorities and public-facing materials that followed.

Research and consultation activities included constituent conversations, policy consultation, a Women’s Group Consultation (Kababaihang ng Bataan tungo sa Kaunlaran) for Advocacy Development, and caucuses across all 46 barangays of Dinalupihan, Bataan.
